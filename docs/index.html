Quadratic Programming for Murabaha Portfolio Risk Management

Abdelwadoud El Khalfi, Asmaa Faris, Asmae Daaou, Mohamed El Gharb, Mostafa El Hachloufi, Soukaina Hlal · Hassan II University, Casablanca

Under revision · Statistics, Optimization & Information Computing

How should a Moroccan participatory bank spread its Murabaha financing across real estate, automobile and equipment? The study builds a transparent, constrained mean-variance allocation from Bank Al-Maghrib's public data. It then tests how much of that allocation survives changed assumptions and an out-of-sample check.

Baseline optimal allocation
62.32%Real estate
10.06%Automobile
27.63%Equipment + raw materials

This allocation maximizes the growth-to-volatility ratio relative to an assumed 4.5% reference rate. At the optimum no bound is active, and the KKT conditions are verified numerically.

Data

The data are month-end outstanding Murabaha financing by sector, in thousand MAD, from Bank Al-Maghrib's monthly reports on participatory banks and windows. Every value was checked against the original reports.

In September 2024 Bank Al-Maghrib reclassified raw-materials financing out of equipment. The baseline adds the two back together, so the series stays consistent over the whole sample. A four-segment version is reported as a robustness check.

Method

The study uses a Markowitz mean-variance model solved by Sequential Least Squares Quadratic Programming (SLSQP). The annualized 3 × 3 covariance matrix is positive definite with condition number 12.3, and the solver converges in 10 iterations. The efficient frontier is computed by solving the constrained problem along a grid of targets. It is not taken from a cloud of random portfolios.

Robustness and out-of-sample test

  • The ranking real estate > equipment > automobile holds across reference rates of 0–4.5%, real-estate ceilings of 50–100%, gap-filling choices and covariance shrinkage.
  • Real estate is the largest weight in 94.0% of block-bootstrap resamples.
  • The exact weights are fragile. The equipment share ranges from 5.4% to 42.4%, depending on the outlier-clipping threshold.

The optimized allocation grows faster than the observed sector mix (26.8% versus 20.0% a year). It does not, however, beat minimum-variance, risk-parity or observed allocations on the growth-to-volatility ratio. The framework is offered as a transparent decision aid for asset-liability committees and Sharia boards, not as a validated optimal policy.

Growth is not return

Bank Al-Maghrib publishes outstanding balances only. The model uses balance growth as a proxy for how each segment performs, so it does not measure profit, default or early repayment. The 4.5% reference rate, the 70% ceiling and the 2% floors are the authors' assumptions. They are not regulatory or Sharia rules.

Replication

The full replication package is public on GitHub: Kh-Abdo/murabaha-portfolio-replication. It contains the raw and corrected data, the code, and one command (python run_all.py) that rebuilds every number, table and figure in about four minutes.
